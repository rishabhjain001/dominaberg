The problem

Roads are inspected by memory,
not by measurement

A complaint arrives with a photo. A beat inspector carries paper. Neither produces a register, a coordinate, or a second look. The gap is not vision — it is the workflow: capture, confirm, georeference, export, repeat.

How it works

One aircraft, one pass, one register

01
Plan
Boustrophedon coverage with 35 % overlap. Lanes, path length and ETA before take-off.
02
Fly
35 m AGL, 8 m/s. 40.4 × 30.3 m footprint, 6.3 cm per pixel.
03
Detect
Carriageway mask, hull geometry and solidity, five classes, on board.
04
Confirm
Two frames must agree within 3 m. The position is the running mean.
05
Export
CSV for the works ledger, GeoJSON for the GIS, markdown for the file.

75-DAY PLAN

Days 1–15 · Field calibration
50 measured defects, thresholds fitted to local asphalt.
Days 16–30 · Flight integration
Pipeline on the companion computer, gimbal, frame timing.
Days 31–45 · Autonomy and safety
Geofence, return to home, wind limits, lost link.
Days 46–60 · Municipal pilot
Two wards, four weekly sorties, engineer review.
Days 61–75 · Handover
GIS import, operator manual, training.

READINESS

TRL 4

Integrated workflow validated in a controlled environment.

TRL 5 needs the airframe and measured defects. TRL 6 needs two wards and a signed pilot report — both inside this 75-day plan.

Software cost: zero. Python, OpenCV, FastAPI, ArduPilot, QGIS.

Perception

Five classes separated by geometry, not by a trained model

POTHOLE
Small, solid, dark. No distinct rim.

hull 0.2-3.8 m²
MISSING COVER
Very dark hole inside a darker rim.

ring ratio > 2.6
WATERLOGGING
Large, solid, dark, low saturation.

hull > 4 m²
DEBRIS
Bright compact pile on asphalt.

0.6-9 m²
PAVEMENT DAMAGE
Thin, sparse crack network.

solid < 0.45
Filled hull
Measures the true footprint, even when only the rim of a large pool responds.
Solidity
Share of the footprint that is genuinely dark. A pool is solid; a crack web is sparse.
Two agreeing frames
Nothing is reported until a second frame lands within three metres.
